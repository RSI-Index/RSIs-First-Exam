1. Executive Summary

Objective

Find one genuinely new optimizer—same equations, grouping, state semantics, and frozen source across E0–E5—that improves Paloma BPB and fixed-window loss over locked AdamH without materially regressing any scale.

Selected scientific candidate

opt-002-rmbt, the full RMBT arm with row blocks, α=1, clamp 8, and vocabulary projection excluded. All six ladder rungs reached their declared scoring updates from source inventory ae30e6f0….

Main quantitative takeaway. The completed ladder achieved step-matched reward 1.00355. Against AdamH, five of six rungs improved both micro BPB and fixed-window loss; E1 regressed slightly (+0.131% micro, +0.284% loss) but remained well inside the scientific 1% non-inferiority allowance. Direct mechanism-on/off comparisons were positive at all five fully paired scales, with geometric gains from 1.00157 to 1.00296.
Main unresolved question. Can RMBT pass the task’s cost-matched staging rules under controlled hardware/runtime conditions? The mechanism is positive at matched steps, including an implementation-matched E5 replication, but cost variance near the steep WSD endpoint flips the formal E5 ablation gate. E4 also lacks a completed scoring-step off-control.

SCIENTIFICALLY VALIDATED NOT FORMALLY STAGED These are not contradictory: the first describes matched-step evidence; the second describes the task’s full set of operational and cost-matched guards. No submission.json was produced.

5. Mechanism Validation

Evidence supporting the mechanism

All five completed scale pairs improve both primary metrics. The E5 mechoffv2 replication, sharing every kernel and differing only by α=0, gives −0.305% micro and gain 1.00302. Applying RMBT to the non-exchangeable vocabulary matrix was harmful (+1.77% fixed-window in the final artifact), as predicted. α=2 over-corrected (+0.437%), while α=1 was strong and principled.

Evidence that weakens or bounds the claim

The effect is small (~0.2–0.3%), and E3 disproves a smooth monotonic scaling interpretation. E4 is incomplete. Intermediate Paloma evaluations were noisy, so endpoint evidence matters. Cost-matched E5 evaluation fails despite matched-step replication, demonstrating sensitivity to restart/runtime cost rather than disproving the update rule.

7. Final Conclusion

Final selected/validated result: top-level HYPOTHESIS.md and NOVELTY.md identify opt-002-rmbt—row-wise Radius-Matched Block Trust, α=1, clamp 8, vocabulary excluded—as the final full arm. It is a complete, frozen-source E0–E5 scientific result, but there is no formally staged submission.

The strongest evidence is the combination of a 1.00355 six-rung matched-step reward, positive mechanism-on/off outcomes at five complete scales, an implementation-matched E5 replication, exact α=0 identity, and predicted failure modes observed when vocabulary rows were included or α was pushed too high.

Caveats are equally clear: the absolute effect is small; E1 is slightly worse than the historical manifest despite the mechanism helping against an in-environment off-control; monotonic scaling is not supported; E4 lacks a final paired control; and cost-matched gates remain sensitive to hardware/restart variance near the WSD endpoint.

Recommended next experiment. Run a predeclared, implementation-identical α=1 versus α=0 paired replication at E1, E4, and E5 on the same hardware allocation and synchronized restart policy, with both matched-step and matched-cost endpoints. This directly tests whether the staging failures disappear when runtime variance is controlled and completes the missing E4 endpoint.

Reading guide: matched-step vs matched-cost

Matched-step compares both algorithms at the same declared training update and is the basis of the scientific reward shown here. Matched-cost compares at equal accumulated GPU-seconds and is used by some staging guards. Near the final WSD decay, even a modest runtime difference can move one arm hundreds of updates earlier, materially changing loss/BPB. The report therefore presents both and never substitutes one for the other.
